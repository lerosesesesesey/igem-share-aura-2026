<Section id="overview" title="Hardware overview" kicker="01">

AURA's physical layer links three jobs: guide milk past a sensing point during milking, expose a functionalised BioFET to the target microRNA, and carry the resulting electrical change to an alert interface. The goal is parlour-friendly screening with minimal disruption to the normal workflow.

</Section>

<Section id="strip" title="Inline milk path" kicker="02">

The concept places a small sensing module in an inline sampling path. Milk moves through tubing and past the sensor during milking, reducing the need for a separate collection-and-test routine.

**Design choices.**
- Food-contact materials and cleanable geometry
- A controlled sensing volume and repeatable flow past the active surface
- A bypass or removable cartridge so screening does not compromise the milk line
- Clear separation between wet fluidics and dry electronics

</Section>

<Section id="reader" title="BioFET and electronics" kicker="03">

The BioFET surface is intended to recognise a mastitis-associated microRNA. Binding changes the field at the transistor surface, producing an electrical response that can be conditioned, digitised, and compared with a validated decision threshold.

<Section id="env" title="Environmental robustness" kicker="04">

Bench tests must cover temperature, milk composition, fouling, cleaning, flow rate, and electrical drift because parlours are not controlled laboratories. Results will feed back into calibration and cartridge-life recommendations.

</Section>

<Section id="integration" title="Integration with workflow" kicker="05">

Hardware design follows the poster workflow: screen during milking, convert the BioFET response into a digital signal, and send a clear alert to a phone or computer. The alert supports a closer check; it is not a diagnosis.

</Section>
